# Long-form script rules (Plan page)

How Claude writes outlines and scripts for the Plan page (`/plan`). The job flow is in
`docs/studio/plans.md`. Three layers, in order of authority:

1. **Bryan's finished scripts** decide how it reads (format, rhythm, faith, CTA).
2. **Joe Aquino's speaking pattern** decides how the lines sound (only how he talks).
3. **Kallaway's framework and story locks** decide the retention mechanics.

## Read before every script

1. `plans/examples/*.md`: three of Bryan's finished scripts (reviews system, 80/20 Meta ads,
   estimates ghosted). **Read at least two in full.** Match them more than anything in this file.
2. `plans/blueprint.md`: business, offer, story, audience, objections. (`plans/` is private, not committed.)
3. `engines/content-os/voice-dna.md`: how Bryan talks, plus "Personality, Drive & Purpose" (Joe's personality).
4. Two Joe Aquino transcripts for his speaking pattern only: `engines/content-os/transcripts/url/style-ref_thejoeaquino-assets-funnel.md`
   and `style-ref_thejoeaquino-trust-recession.md`. Borrow how he talks, never his clients, numbers or stories.
5. `styles/SCRIPT-SHAPES.md` "Cue tags".

## The format (from Bryan's finished scripts)

```
# <video title>
## INTRO
<spoken lines>
## ACT 1 — <WHAT THIS ACT PROVES, IN CAPS>
<spoken lines>
### <SUB-SECTION IN CAPS>          (optional: an analogy, a template, "THE STEP MOST CONTRACTORS SKIP")
<spoken lines>
## <STANDALONE SECTION IN CAPS>    (sections between acts are fine: "THE REAL PROBLEM", "MAKE IT STUPID SIMPLE")
...
## ACT 3 — <...>
## <RECAP SECTION>                 (e.g. "THE COMPLETE 80/20 SYSTEM", "REVIEWS COMPOUND")
## CTA
<spoken lines>
```

- 6 to 10 sections, about 1,800 to 3,000 spoken words (12 to 20 minutes). Headings name the idea or
  the search term ("HIGHER-INCOME ZIP CODE TARGETING IN META ADS"), never "Section 3".
- **One spoken sentence per line.** Short lines, fragments allowed: "Tools put away. Materials stacked. Site left clean."
- On-screen lists (menu paths, form options, ad set setups) are `* ` bullet lines, read out loud.
- Stage notes go on their own line in square brackets: `[5 sec pause]`, `[show Google Maps search]`.
- No "Open Loop" / "Payoff" labels in the script: the loops are written into the lines.

## How it sounds: Joe Aquino's speaking pattern

Only Joe's way of talking, layered onto Bryan's voice. The structure, content and teaching stay
Bryan's and Kallaway's. Never borrow Joe's clients, numbers, stories or offers.

- **Talks straight to the viewer, in the moment:** "I'm going to show you…", "I'm going to give it to
  you right here, really quick." "Many of you…" "Most of you are missing this."
- **Asks, then answers:** "Now how do we do this?" "So what does that mean for you?" Rhetorical
  questions in a run of two or three when the point matters: "Is that worth it? Is that worth your weekends?"
- **Tell-and-show phrasing:** "not just tell you, but show you." "Here's the proof." "You can see it right here."
- **Punchy contrast pairs:** "Attract, don't convince. Serve, don't sell." "It's not about what. It's about who."
- **Repetition for weight:** "over and over and over again." "Same process. Every time."
- **Short verdict lines after a point:** "That's the power of that." "It's the same process." "That's the whole game."
- **Conversational glue:** "right?", "Again,", "So again, if you really want to…", "Because…". Use
  sparingly so it reads natural, never every line.
- **Conviction, not hedging:** says it like it's true. Faith said plainly and warmly, the way Joe does,
  with Bryan's own verses and stories.
- **Energy:** quick, lean sentences that keep moving. Read it out loud: if it sounds like an essay, cut it down.

## Bryan's voice (from his finished scripts)

- **Intro, one of two openers:** (a) a short personal story from his dad's contracting business that
  turns into the lesson ("Growing up, I watched my dad clean the job site…"), or (b) a direct problem
  callout ("You're getting leads. You're driving out to projects. And then, after the estimate,
  nothing."). Then the reframe ("So you assume your ads aren't working. But they are. It's your sales
  process."), the system map, a tease of something at the end ("at the end, I'll give you the exact
  message…"), credibility from the blueprint, and "Let's get into it."
- **Faith, once per act, tied back to the work.** A verse with its reference, or a short Bible story
  told simply (the Parable of the Talents, Noah's measurements, Jesus and timing), then straight back
  to the business: "The point is simple: …" or "And I think this applies to contractors, too."
  Never preachy, never more than a few lines except one longer story per video. End on stewardship.
- **Do it now.** Most sections end with an action the viewer takes today: "Check your Google Business
  Profile right now." "Save that message as a template in your phone right now."
- **Worked numbers.** Napkin math with real-looking examples the viewer can follow ($30 a day → $24
  and $6; premium $48K, standard $29K, basic $18K). Example math is fine; client results must be real.
- **Simple analogies** a kid would get: the package that gets verified, packed, tracked and handed
  off; the $50 toy that makes the $20 toy look cheap.
- **Client stories** ("I had a client, a landscaper, sitting at 6 reviews…") only from Bryan's notes
  or blueprint. Otherwise `[PLACEHOLDER: client story with real numbers]`.
- **Honest caveats:** "availability can vary by account", "a starting framework, not a guarantee",
  "Don't assume the broad audience will automatically be worse. Watch the actual results."
- **Engagement deal** (optional): "So here's the deal between me and you…"
- **CTA:** tie the topic back to the full contractor growth system (Google profile, website,
  follow-up, CRM, ads, reviews working together), then point to the next video, by default the
  "$500K a year contracting business" video. Optionally "subscribe".

## Retention mechanics (Kallaway)

- **Intro:** confirm the click in the first line, state the common belief, flip it (voice-dna's
  "Most contractors think X, but it's Y"), then the plan and the proof.
- **Order:** second-best point first, best point second, then the rest.
- **Every point:** what it is, how to do it, why it matters to the bigger picture.
- **Bridges:** the last line of each section opens the next ("But fast follow-up to the wrong person
  is still wasted time. So who actually deserves an estimate?"). Use a contrast word: but, actually,
  instead, turns out.
- **Story locks:** name one or two frameworks ("the 80/20 system", "the package analogy"); no hedging
  (never if, maybe, might, could, probably as a claim); one or two "you're probably thinking…" lines
  answering a blueprint objection; at least one negative frame ("This is where most guys burn their money").
- **Recap** before the CTA, listing the parts in order.

## Cue tags for the edit

At the end of the spoken line they belong to: `[HOOK]` on the first intro line, `[CHAPTER: <short
name>]` on the first line of each `##` section after the intro, `[CTA]` on the first CTA line,
`[NUMBER: …]` on figures worth showing, `[STEP: n]` on steps, `[PROOF: …]` where a screenshot proves
it, `[BROLL: …]` for a shot to cut to, `[EMPHASIS: word]` on a big line's key word. At least one visual
tag about every 75 spoken words. Stage notes (`[5 sec pause]`) stay lowercase so they aren't read as tags.

## Honesty

Never invent client names, results, testimonials, screenshots or statistics. Bryan has no client
testimonials: never claim any. Use what's in the plan's notes and the blueprint; everything else is a
`[PLACEHOLDER: what real fact goes here]`. Don't promise viewers specific income. Keep platform facts
(Google, Meta, Angi) accurate, with a caveat where they vary.

## Outline (the "Outline & check" button)

Write `plans/<id>/outline.md`:

```
# <video title>
## Packaging angles
### How can I make this appeal to someone who hasn't heard about <topic>?
### How can I make this appeal to someone who is a beginner?
### How can I make this appeal to someone advanced?
### How can I make this appeal to my mom?   (a simple visual or motion-graphic idea)
## Proof to open with
<the real result, or [PLACEHOLDER] saying what Bryan needs to supply>
## The system map
<every part, in order, in one line>
## Common belief → contrarian take
## Sections
### 1. <SECTION NAME> (second-best point)
<what / how / why it matters, the do-it-now action, the verse or story>
### 2. <SECTION NAME> (best point)
...
## The missing piece
<the part most contractors skip>
## Uniqueness check
<per section: NEW or COMMON, and for COMMON a sharper angle>
## Native offer moment
<where Elivate is the natural solve, or "none">
```

When a script is requested and an outline exists, follow its sections and order, and sharpen anything marked COMMON.
