# The played-share variant — measured layout mix (THE default)

**Source:** REF-B, a 13-minute public tutorial on a design tool, 1920×1080,
30fps, 812s, built from rebuilt UI demos rather than screen recordings. Its
frames are not shipped with this kit; only the measurements are.

This is the **target mix for every video this skill makes.** It replaces
REF-A's 51/49 camera/canvas split. REF-A's camera move, card geometry and
caption measurements still apply; the layout mix, the ground and the canvas
motion come from here.

⚠ That video is another creator's work. Take the **mix and the technique**.
Their ground colour, card shape, title face, logo and copy all stay theirs.

---

## 1 · Method

Every frame at 5fps (4,058 frames), classified automatically and then checked by
eye on a random 16-frame contact sheet per class (all four came back clean):

- **Face size** with an OpenCV face detector. The sizes split into two clean
  clusters at 960px width: **60–90px** (small card) and **110–140px** (big card
  or full frame). Nothing fell between 90 and 110.
- **Ground fraction**: the share of pixels within 14 levels of the measured ground
  colour. Under 0.2 means footage (or a web page) fills the frame.
- **Canvas motion** at 10fps: the share of non-camera pixels changing by more than
  12 levels between frames, with the camera card masked out (a talking head
  doesn't count as animation).
- A 1.4s majority filter removes detector blips before runs are counted.

## 2 · Share of runtime (the target)

| Layout | `cam` in spec.py | What it is | Share | Median hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FULL** | `FULL` | camera full-bleed (often a chapter opener) | **16%** | 4.4s |
| **HALF** | `HALF` | big camera card (560×740) on one side, UI on the other | **39%** | 9.1s |
| **DEMO** | `CORNER` | small camera card (300×300) in a corner, big UI doing the demo | **35%** | 8.0s |
| **PAGE** | `CORNER` + `box="ss-bleedsafe"` | a full-width web page with the card over the corner | **4%** | one 33s run |
| **NO CAM** | — | a full-screen graphic: chapter card, document, intro title | **6%** | 6.8s |

**Camera on screen ≈ 94% of runtime. Full-frame camera only 16%.** Most of the
video, the viewer is looking at a real interface with the creator beside it.

The camera card sits **left 48% / right 52%** and swaps sides between beats,
so the frame never looks the same for long (`camera_side: "swap"` in
brand.json; `left` / `right` pin it).

## 3 · Cadence

- **101 layout changes in 812s: one every 8.0s.**
- **A big visual change (over 8% of the canvas in one frame) every 4.9s** on
  top of that: the content swaps inside a layout about once between layout
  changes.
- FULL beats are short (median 4.4s, max 11.8s). They are breathers and chapter
  openers, not where the explaining happens.

## 4 · How much is animated

The share of time something on the canvas is visibly moving:

| Layout | moving |
|---|---|
| HALF | 48% |
| DEMO | 36% |
| PAGE | 13% |
| NO CAM | 55% |
| **All graphics time** | **42%** |

So this is **not** REF-A's static canvas. Aim for **about 40% of graphics time
in motion**: things arrive, the cursor moves, text types, a menu opens, a page
scrolls. For the other ~60% the UI **holds still** so it can be read.
`scripts/check-static.py` will report motion on this variant — that is
expected; it is only a gate for the `classic` ground.

## 5 · What the "screen share" actually is

Stepped at 2fps: **the UI is a rebuilt mock, not a recording.** A simplified app
window with only the elements being talked about, and a scripted cursor that:
- moves to the address bar, where the URL types itself in with a focus ring
- clicks a pill, and a dropdown opens
- clicks through to a new screen with a short blur cross-fade (~3 frames)
- types a description letter by letter
- drops file chips into an upload well

Every "open X, go to Y, click Z" line in the transcript gets this treatment.
For public sites it uses real page captures with a cursor on them (a 33s PAGE
run). Prompts the viewer is told to send are shown as a prompt box with the
text typing in.

## 6 · Planning rule

Plan the layout track in `PLAN.md` against these numbers, and check the build
output (build.py prints the mix) against them:

- FULL 14–18% · HALF 36–42% · CORNER 32–38% · PAGE + NO CAM about 10% together
- a layout change every 7–9s; a canvas swap about every 5s
- the camera card switches sides between beats (unless pinned)
- about 40% of graphics time in motion

## 6b · How a played screen share is built

1. **One demo per beat, as its own HyperFrames project** in `demos/<name>/`,
   generated by `demos/make_demos.py` (helpers in `demos/demolib.py`). Each demo
   is a rebuilt UI (browser chrome, an app composer, an editor, …) with a
   scripted cursor: `mv`, `click` (with a ripple), `type` (character by
   character), `show`/`hide`. It all runs on one paused timeline, so it's
   seek-safe.
2. **Time it in demo-local seconds**: the word onset in `transcript-tight.json`
   minus the beat's start. Things land ON the word (a file appears when it is
   named).
3. **Render each demo to `assets/broll/<name>.mp4`** (`scripts/render-demos.sh`),
   then place it with `panel(..., loop=False)`. One demo can serve two beats
   (HALF, then CORNER) with `media_start`.
4. **Real sites are captured, not rebuilt.** Take a tall CDP capture
   (`scripts/capture.js`, mode `tall`) into `assets/captures/` and scroll it
   inside the rebuilt browser (`d3_page` in make_demos.py). That's frame-exact,
   where a live screencast gives uneven frame timing.
5. **Rebuild the real product against CURRENT screenshots** when the creator
   names one. Never invent features, buttons or prices it does not have.
6. **The demos are media, not the composition.** They carry the app's own
   colours, so `check-screenshare.sh` exempts `demos/` from the token rule only.
   They are still checked for emojis and placeholder copy.
7. **A "bad" example is one you make yourself**, labelled on screen as made
   for this video. Never hold up a real company's work as the bad one.
8. **Start a camera move ~0.3s before the phrase.** Speech-to-text often reports
   0.000s gaps, and a move starting on the onset delays that caption under the
   minimum hold, so it gets dropped.
9. **Cap CORNER and PAGE panels at 740px tall**, like HALF. Taller and the
   caption touches the panel's bottom edge.

## 7 · What gets demoed

The creator does not record screen shares. The edit plays them, and it has to
feel natural, like a real share:
- **Any tool or app they name** (an AI app, settings, a menu): rebuild the
  screen and walk the cursor through the exact path they say.
- **Any website they talk about**: show the real site, captured from the live
  page, with a cursor on it and a scroll where it helps.
- **Qualities they talk about** ("beautiful design", "bad design", "slow site"):
  find real examples of the good one and show them; make the bad one yourself.
  Pair them when the creator contrasts them.

How much of this sourcing happens is set by the **research level** in
brand.json (SKILL.md §3).

## 8 · The live ground

MEASURED off REF-B:
- A flat warm off-white ground (yours comes from brand.json: bright paper,
  dark, or `classic` white with the field switched off).
- A dot matrix on a **12px pitch** covering the lower part of the frame. Its
  top edge sits around y 470–560.
- Each column is a bar. **Its top dot is a bigger, darker cap**, and the dots
  under it start small and grow with depth.
- The bar heights form **wide, flat terraces** (low spatial frequency). They
  drift slowly, about 1 row per 0.3s at the fastest columns. Traced at 10fps,
  the skyline **never shifts sideways**: it breathes in place.

Build: `class="ss-frame ss-live"` plus `<canvas class="ss-liveground">`, drawn
every frame by `drawGround(t)` in `build.py` as a pure function of absolute time
(seek-exact, and seamless across segments). Tokens `--ss-live-*` in
`assets/tokens.css`; the cap colour picks up a whisper of your accent.

⚠ Keep the spatial frequencies low (0.04–0.16 rad per column). Higher values
read as jagged spikes, nothing like the reference's terraces.

⚠ Anything that must be read sits on a card or in a share window, never
directly on the dot skyline.
