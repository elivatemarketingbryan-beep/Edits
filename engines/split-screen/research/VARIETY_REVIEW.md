## Actionable design rules

1. **Choose medium and format from meaning.** Write the beat's visual purpose before its colors: demonstration, inspection, transformation, comparison, selection, mechanism, or direct human address.
2. **Vary the construction within the edit where the idea calls for it.** Real footage, focused UI crops, tactile objects, layered illustration, expressive type, galleries, and presenter shots can coexist. They need clear transitions and narrative continuity, not identical surfaces.
3. **Do not recolor the same cards.** If a storyboard remains a repeated set of cards, icons, pills, and arrows after removing the words, changing yellow to pastel has not addressed the feedback. Change the dominant object, medium, depth, arrangement, or action.
4. **Keep understandable anchors.** Speech captions, factual meaning, readable scale, and deliberate presenter framing provide continuity. Typefaces, materials, backgrounds, and palettes can vary around those anchors.
5. **Let objects behave according to the idea.** A lens inspects, paper receives marks, a gallery selects, an interface reveals a real detail, and a process changes state. Use staged reveals with time to inspect the result. Avoid idle float as the main animation.
6. **Source real subject assets.** Use verified official product images, repository or UI screenshots, and logos where useful. Download locally, inspect them, and record provenance off screen. Use original diagrams for concepts that do not have a real asset.
7. **Preserve the user's no-boilerplate direction.** No on-screen “illustrative,” “not real,” sourcing footers, or gray provenance microcopy. Keep honest conceptual visuals and factual boundaries; this is not permission to invent measurements, quotations, or product results.
8. **Treat white/black/yellow as an option.** No global yellow, pastel, dark, or grid requirement follows from this sample. Color supports the selected medium and concept.

## Applying this to the MiroFish edit

These are proposed original constructions, not claims that they appear in Jack's reels: a crowd or network can explain scale; a real repository crop can establish the open-source project; distinct launch-page/product/ad/offer artifacts can converge as inputs; behavior or perspective can be shown through contrasting human/object arrangements; an offer can visibly change after feedback; the final invitation can return to a presenter-led composition with strong display type. Choose among them based on available source evidence and the actual speech. Avoid forcing every beat into the same card row, avatar grid, or palette.
