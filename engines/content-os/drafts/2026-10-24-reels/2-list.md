## L01 · Ranking every lead source for contractors, S to F
**Edit style:** Ranking · **Shape:** List / ranking · **Runtime:** ~1:50
**Hook:** "I'm ranking every lead source contractors use, from best to worst."
**Beats**
1. Open on the promise to rank them honestly, with one line on what you're judging: how ready the homeowner is, not volume.
2. Go through seven sources, two or three sentences each, and say the tier out loud every time.
3. Put the surprise in the middle (shared leads near the bottom), not at the end.
4. Close on "the best sources are the ones you own" and the follow CTA.
**Delivery:** Fast and decisive. Each verdict is a beat; pause half a second after saying the tier.
**Script**
```text
I'm ranking every lead source contractors use, from best to worst. [HOOK]
And I'm judging them on one thing, how ready that homeowner is to actually hire you.
Not how many leads you get. How many turn into signed jobs.
Referrals. Someone they trust already vouched for you. S tier. [TIER: S]
They show up pre-sold, they argue less on price, and they send you the next one.
Google Maps. They searched for exactly what you do, in your town, today. S tier. [TIER: S]
Nobody searches "roof replacement near me" for fun. They've got a problem and they want it fixed.
Your own website with real reviews. A tier, as long as it's built to book. [TIER: A]
If it has a call button, real photos and reviews up top, it closes. If it's a brochure, it doesn't.
Meta ads. Great when the system behind them is set up, rough when it's not. B tier. [TIER: B]
Ads create the demand. But without fast follow-up, you're just paying for names.
Yard signs and truck wraps. Good for being seen, slow for the phone ringing. C tier. [TIER: C]
They build recognition over time, they just don't fill next week's calendar.
Shared leads from Angi or HomeAdvisor. The same homeowner sold to other contractors at the same time. D tier. [TIER: D]
```
