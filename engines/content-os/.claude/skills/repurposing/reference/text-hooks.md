# Text hooks: the first line of a caption, post, title or subject line

For spoken video hooks use the `hook-generator` skill (the 404-hook bank). This file is for
written first lines: IG captions, carousel slide 1, YouTube community posts, titles, email subjects.

The first line has one job: buy the second line. Write it **after** you know what the piece says;
a hook written first becomes a promise the body can't keep.

**The hook must be true and the body must pay it off.** If a claim has to be invented to make the
hook work, the hook is wrong, or the idea is.

## Six patterns

| Pattern | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Contradiction | Two true things that shouldn't both be true | "Best month we ever had. Almost missed payroll." |
| Specific number | A real figure with a stake attached | "40% of the cash crunches I see happen in a best quarter." |
| Reader's own thought | Say what they're already thinking | "You checked your balance this morning and felt fine." |
| Named mistake | The error, stated flatly | "Contractors don't go broke from slow months. They go broke from fast ones." |
| Cost of not knowing | What the ignorance costs | "The deposit isn't your money. Spending it is how good years end." |
| Concrete moment | Drop the reader into a scene | "March. Payroll runs Friday. $11,400 in the bank, $19,000 owed." |

Avoid: questions that answer themselves ("Want more clients?"), "Here's the thing", "Let me tell you
a story", and any opener that announces what the post is about.

## Procedure

1. State the idea in one plain sentence, for yourself.
2. Find the truest specific detail in the source: a number, date, dollar figure, moment. From the
   transcript, never from imagination.
3. Write five hooks across different patterns.
4. Cut each to its shortest true form (most improve by deleting the first four words).
5. Keep the best one plus one alternate in the draft file.

## Three tests

- **Scroll test:** mid-feed, no name attached, would it stop a contractor? Not everyone, that audience.
- **Payoff test:** does the body deliver exactly what the hook implies?
- **Voice test:** could this line have opened one of Bryan's own posts (`voice-dna.md`, `voice-corpus/`)?

## Per format

- **IG caption:** the first ~125 characters show before "more". The hook must fit there.
- **Carousel slide 1:** 6-10 words, the hook alone on the slide.
- **Reel on-screen text:** different words from the spoken hook. Two hooks, one idea.
- **YouTube title:** under 60 characters, specific noun over wordplay, matches the thumbnail without repeating it.
- **YouTube community post:** the first line is the whole hook; the poll or image does the rest.
- **Email subject:** 4-7 words; the preview text is a second hook, not a repeat.

A number the transcript only approximates ("about eleven grand") gets `[NEED: confirm]` before it
becomes "$11,400" in public.
