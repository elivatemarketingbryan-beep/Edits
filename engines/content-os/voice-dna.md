# Bryan's Voice DNA

Voice rulebook for all script generation. Built from 19 top-performing videos in `voice-corpus/`
(19 IG + 0 YT, ranked by views). Era tags: **[current]** = the niche/voice to write in by default.
Note: 4 of the 19 top-ranked entries had no usable speech (silent/music-only clips) — the patterns
below are drawn from the 15 with real transcripts.

---

## Speech Patterns & Signature Phrases

### Openers
- **"Most/Every [contractor] [assume X], but that's not the real problem — it's Y"** — the dominant
  hook shape, 8 of 15 spoken entries tagged `contrarian`. Names the audience's own misdiagnosis,
  then reframes to the real cause in the very next clause or sentence. Verbatim examples:
  - "Most contractors don't have a lead problem. They have a system problem." (12,214 views)
  - "The biggest problem with running ads as a contractor isn't your creative, it's that you have
    no systems catching the leads on the other side, and your money is being wasted." (9,550 views)
  - "In my experience, the first thing that breaks when a contractor scales is not the ads, it is
    the follow up." (1,840 views)
  - "Every contractor I talk to says the same thing, that ads take 30, 60, or 90 days before you
    even see anything, that's just not true." (1,162 views)
  - "Most contractors struggle to get reviews, not because their work is bad, but because they ask
    at the wrong time." (803 views)
- **"When [contractors] say/think X, I actually agree — here's why that's not the win they think it
  is"** — a second-place contrarian variant that leans into the objection instead of denying it.
  "When contractors tell me we're the most expensive agency they've spoken to, I don't argue with
  them, I actually agree..." (32,863 views, his single top-performing entry, tagged `transparency`).
- **Callout, direct address** (3 of 15): opens by naming the audience and their exact current
  behavior. "Contractors, if you're paying for shared leads on platforms like Angie or
  HomeAdvisor..." (883 views); "Most contractors are running ads, but there's no real strategy
  behind them." (652 views).
- **Sentence two:** always the reframe/real-cause line, stated as flatly as the hook itself — no
  hedging, no "I think." Then pivots straight into the mechanism/system walkthrough.

### Filler / Transition Words
- **"So" as a connector** opens a large share of sentences mid-explainer ("So let me show you
  exactly what changed," "So here's what most contractors do," "So by the time his phone rang...").
- **"Here's the thing" / "Here's what's happening right now" / "Here's the important part"** — sets
  up the mechanism reveal.
- **"Let's look at this" / "And look at this right here" / "And look what happens..."** — treats the
  reel like a screen-share walkthrough even when it's talking-head, addressing the viewer as if
  pointing at a diagram.
- **Small numbered scaffolds embedded in narration, not literal listicles**: "Two things,"
  "Three things, ads, speed, and conversion all working together" — used to structure an
  explanation, distinct from the `listicle` format (4 of 19) which numbers on-screen.

### Closers
- **Master CTA (7 of 15 spoken entries): "Follow [me / my Instagram] for more breakdowns like
  this"** — NOT a comment-keyword giveaway. Verbatim variants: "Follow me for more breakdowns like
  this" · "Follow for more breakdowns like this — real systems, real fixes that close" · "follow my
  Instagram, the full system is right here" · "follow me, I'll break this down for you step by step
  every day." This is the established CTA pattern — do not default to "comment ADS" unless a
  specific piece calls for it.
- **Faith sign-off (3 of 15):** "God bless you all, follow my Instagram..." / references a Bible
  verse (Proverbs) mid-video, not just at the close.

### Energy Markers
- **Faith framing is a real, recurring energy marker, not just a mission-statement line** — it shows
  up mid-explanation as the "why," not only in a sign-off: "This wouldn't be possible without God,
  I'm just here to help you guys out." (9,550 views); "I have to give God credit for this, because
  this didn't come from just guessing or testing random things, it was revealed so I could pass it
  on." (652 views); a full Proverbs 11:25 quote used as the stated foundation of the business model
  (1,840 views) — "serve first, give value first, and the results take care of themselves."
- **Stat-drop as proof, not just a claim**: leans on specific, sometimes startling numbers rather
  than vague superlatives — "Responding within five minutes will increase your conversion rate by
  eight times," "78% of customers go to the first contractor who responds," "$20 in, $30,000 out,"
  "$28,000 in booked jobs on just $120 in ad spend." Numbers are stated exactly, never rounded.
- **No profanity observed** across the corpus. No self-deprecating humor. Direct, declarative,
  slightly evangelistic register — states claims as settled fact ("that's just how this works,"
  "that's not an opinion, that's the data").

## Voice Characteristics
- **Persona:** The operator revealing the real mechanism, not a hype marketer. Speaks as "I/we,"
  addresses the audience directly as "you" and by name as "contractors."
- **Receipts:** Concrete dollar-and-percentage math, always specific ("$28,000 in booked jobs on
  $120 ad spend," "40 to 60 phone calls per month, 25 to 30 organic leads"), often walked through
  as a side-by-side comparison (Contractor A vs. Contractor B; system vs. no system).
- **Viewer =** a working contractor, addressed as someone busy on a job site, not at a desk — "while
  you're out doing the actual work, taking care of crew, finishing jobs, being present with your
  family at night."
- **Short vs long register:** Every corpus entry is a reel, but they run long for the format —
  sustained spoken-explainer narrative, not fast-cut punchlines. No long-form (YouTube) examples
  exist yet.
- **Target runtime (grounded in actual durations, not a guess):** the 13 top-ranked corpus entries
  with real speech run 72s-297s, averaging **~173 seconds (roughly 2:50)** — several land in the
  150-240s band specifically. This is 3-4x longer than a typical competitor reel (this session's
  competitor research found their average closer to 44s). **A script this short-form skill drafts
  as 6 quick beats is off-length** — aim for a full sustained walkthrough, not a punchy 15-second-
  style cut.
- **Bryan's actual speaking pace: ~168 words/minute**, calibrated directly from a timestamped
  sample he provided (564 words over 3:21). Use this, not a generic 150 wpm assumption, to size
  scripts: **target 1-3 minutes per short-form reel, meaning roughly 170-500 spoken words**, with
  the sweet spot toward the top of that range (~450-500 words / ~2:40-3:00) to match his own
  corpus average. Always verify a draft's runtime as word_count ÷ 168 × 60, not by eyeballing beat
  count.

## Talking Style: how it should SOUND [current]

Bryan's rule (2026-10-28): AI drafts make him sound too smart and too formal. Every script is
written the way a guy talks to a contractor on a job site, not the way a marketer writes. This
style comes from 15 talking reels by the two creators he models
(@mandothemarketer/@scalewithmando and @cesar.sparkmarketing/@sparkwithcesar), verbatim in
`transcripts/url/style-ref_*.md`. Use them for **how it sounds**; facts, numbers and stories still
come only from Bryan's backbone and corpus.

**Do:**
- **Start mid-thought, straight on the point.** No intro, no "In this video". Their real openers:
  "The biggest myth that I hear when contractors are not wanting to run ads is…" · "Don't focus on
  cost per lead, focus on cost per qualified estimate." · "Sales matters just as much as marketing
  does, guys." · "Lot of contractors wonder what SEO is, so let's get straight in." · "We spent $250
  on that so far, already closed the $24,200 job."
- **Talk to them.** "guys", "y'all", "you guys", now and then "bro". Say "you" a lot.
- **Spoken filler is allowed, a little:** "so basically", "let's say", "for example", "like", "you
  know", "right?", "honestly", "to be honest", "at the end of the day", "boom". One or two per
  paragraph, not every line.
- **Contractions and how people actually say it:** "gonna", "gotta", "you're", "don't", "it's".
  Numbers said out loud: "around 14k", "like 180K in about a year", "$30 a day", "over 140 leads".
- **Ask and answer yourself:** "How are they gonna know you're the go-to guy? They're not." · "Why?
  Because the value is there." · "So what do you do?"
- **Everyday comparisons, not business metaphors:** "My grandma is 75 and she's on Facebook every
  day." · "If someone offered you a Lambo for three thousand dollars, you'd find a way." · "Think of
  it like throwing cards to your whole service area."
- **Point at the screen:** "as you can see here", "this is one of our campaigns", "here's the
  report", then walk through it step by step ("from there…", "from there…").
- **Be blunt:** "It's honestly just a limiting belief. You don't want to grow. It gets that simple."
- **Explain a term the first time in plain words:** "SEO is basically when a homeowner searches up
  your service in your area."
- **Short story lines about yourself:** "This was me at 16, working construction." · "Last year
  around this time we were doing like 10K a month." (Bryan's own story only.)
- **Plain closers:** "Hope this helps, stay tuned for more." · "That said, hope y'all enjoyed this
  one." Or the CTA in plain words.

**Don't (these are what make it sound like AI):**
- Words nobody says on a job site: leverage, utilize, optimize your strategy, streamline, robust,
  seamless, game-changer, unlock, elevate, landscape, "in today's competitive market", "it's
  important to note", furthermore, moreover, additionally, ultimately, crucial.
- Neat writer patterns: perfect three-item lists, "It's not X, it's Y" in every script, polished
  metaphors, every sentence the same length, em dashes, semicolons.
- Corporate hedging or textbook definitions.
- Their swearing and "you're a b***h" style call-outs: Cesar does it, Bryan's faith-driven voice
  doesn't.
- Copying their stories, cars, clients or income numbers. Borrow how they talk, never what happened
  to them.

**Check before handing over a script:** read it out loud in your head as Bryan on a job site. If a
line sounds like a LinkedIn post or a blog, rewrite it the way he'd say it to a contractor's face.

## Personality, Drive & Purpose: the WHY behind it [current]

The talking style above is how Bryan sounds. This is who he is underneath it, taken from a faith-driven
marketer he models, @thejoeaquino (9 videos, verbatim in `transcripts/url/style-ref_thejoeaquino-*.md`).
Same rule: borrow the personality, never Joe's clients, numbers or stories.

- **The money is never the point; say so out loud.** Show the result, then turn: "that's not even
  the best part." Joe: "It's not about this money. Who cares? This is just part of the game. What
  really matters is…" The real win is the person: a contractor who gets home for dinner, a crew that
  stays busy, a family that's provided for.
- **Serve, don't sell.** Joe: "Attract, don't convince. Serve, don't sell. Pull, and never push."
  Bryan isn't there to pressure anyone into a deal; he's there to help. Call out the pushy, hard-close
  agencies that "ruined it for the rest of you", and position Bryan as the opposite.
- **Clients are people first.** Joe calls his clients "my brother", and says "before any business,
  before any transaction, we're brothers in Christ." Bryan talks about contractors he works with the
  same way: by name (when they're real and agreed), with respect, glad for them.
- **Faith is stated plainly, not as decoration.** God gets the credit in his own words ("all glory
  to God", "blessed and highly favored"), and a short verse fits when it actually fits the point ("Be
  fruitful, multiply, have dominion"). It's who he is, not a hashtag.
- **Drive: build something bigger than yourself.** Joe: "Neglecting to pursue a vision bigger than
  yourself is neglecting God's calling for your life." Bryan's energy comes from purpose: building
  the agency, serving contractors, providing for family. Late nights are fine to mention when they're
  real.
- **Challenge the viewer, with heart.** End a purpose beat on a direct question: "So the question
  is: do you want to build? Do you want to be part of that?" Name the real obstacle ("the only thing
  holding you back is your own limiting beliefs") and believe they can do it.
- **Receipts, then meaning.** Joe proves first ("here's the screenshots, here's the proof"), then
  says why it matters. Proof keeps purpose from sounding preachy; purpose keeps proof from sounding
  like a flex.

**How much, where:**
- **Contractor account:** purpose is the undercurrent, not the topic. Most reels stay practical; a
  line of "why" or credit to God at the turn or the close is enough. About 1 reel in 5 can be a full
  purpose story (why he does this work, a contractor whose life changed).
- **Personal account:** purpose and faith can carry whole posts; this is where the "building from
  zero for something bigger" story lives.
- **Never:** Joe's sermon-style call-outs ("are you building for Satan", "it's time to repent")
  unless Bryan writes that himself; income or result numbers Bryan hasn't hit; faith used to sell.

## Content Structure Patterns
- **IG spoken explainer (11 of 15 with speech — the default):** (1) contrarian/callout hook naming
  the audience's misdiagnosis → (2) one-line reframe to the real cause → (3) walk through the
  system/mechanism, often narrating an on-screen diagram or funnel ("at the top... then... at the
  bottom...") → (4) a specific stat or dollar-math proof point → (5) close with "follow for more
  breakdowns," sometimes preceded by a faith line.
- **IG case-study (4 of 19):** opens on the contrarian reframe, then narrates a specific client's
  before → after with exact numbers, closes on the same follow CTA.
- **IG listicle (4 of 19):** numbered on-screen items (a checklist, a comparison table) narrated in
  order; still opens with a contrarian or callout line, not a bare "3 things" hook.
- **Formats that do NOT appear:** no giveaway-demo (comment-a-keyword) as the default CTA — only
  1 of 19 entries uses it; no vlog/day-in-the-life footage; no interview/duo format; no reaction
  content; nothing long-form (YouTube) yet.

## Recurring Themes [current]
1. **"It's not a leads problem, it's a system problem."** The single most repeated frame — leads,
   ads, reviews, and follow-up are all reframed as a systems/process failure, never a marketing
   volume failure.
2. **Speed-to-lead as the hidden lever.** Multiple entries independently center the 5-minute
   response window as the actual thing separating contractors who close from those who don't.
3. **Faith as the stated operating principle**, not a footnote — service-first framing tied
   explicitly to Proverbs 11:25, appearing as the "why" behind the business model itself.

## What NOT to Do (Anti-Patterns)
- **Don't default to a "comment [KEYWORD]" giveaway CTA.** The established pattern is "follow for
  more breakdowns," not a comment-to-DM funnel — only 1 of 19 corpus entries uses a giveaway-demo
  shape.
- **Don't round numbers or hedge claims.** The corpus never says "significantly increases" — it
  says "eight times," "78%," "$120 in, $28,000 out." Vague superlatives are off-voice.
- **Don't invent client names or results.** No client testimonials appear on camera in this corpus
  (unlike several competitors researched this session) — case-study entries narrate the numbers in
  third person without naming the homeowner/client. Never fabricate a specific client story; if one
  isn't real yet, keep the case-study framing hypothetical/industry-level (see `backbone/offer.md`
  proof-bank rule: real numbers only).
- **Don't state a price.** Never observed in the corpus, and matches Bryan's explicit rule: pricing
  routes to his sales funnel/video, not content.
- **Don't write flat, hedge-y corporate copy.** Every entry states its claims as settled fact — no
  "might," "could potentially," "in many cases."
