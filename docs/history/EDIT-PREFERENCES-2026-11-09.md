# Edit preferences: what Bryan wants from an edit

Claude's memory of Bryan's editing taste. Read it before the `formats` stage (ideas and
combos) and before every `edit` and `revise`. Rules here override STYLE-GUIDE.md,
the catalog recipes and the combo levels in CLAUDE.md.

When Bryan says how he wants edits done ("too much", "more of this", "never do that"), add
it here, newest first, one entry each:
`YYYY-MM-DD · applies to · Rule. (Why, in his words.)`

---

2026-11-09 · every video · Build suspense through what each shot gives away. Every b-roll shot, graphic and scene must
raise a question or answer one; if it does neither, cut it. Tease a number or result before showing it, and open a graphic
on one detail before pulling out to the whole. Don't run two shots or graphics in a row that give the same information
(a graphic acting out the line he's saying is fine; that rule stands). Pose new questions as old ones close, and leave one open
at the end. Before rendering, read `sheet.json` top to bottom: each scene's `what` should add something new. (Bryan approved
this from Film Editing Pro's "How Professional Editors Build Suspense"; breakdown in REFERENCES.md.)

2026-11-08 · live b-roll · Film burns and a film look on b-roll, and mix the strength: bold amber burns going into a
b-roll stretch and on a couple of key cuts, soft edge leaks on the rest. Never flat white, never blotchy. Graphics and wipes
must not clip the start or end of a live shot. (Bryan: "add film burns and effects to the b roll", "some subtler and some not".)

2026-11-08 · re-edits · When re-editing a finished video, ask before removing an original animation with real interaction in it;
Bryan may want it rebuilt in the new style rather than replaced. Layouts should open centered and slide aside to make room for
text, not start off to one side. (Bryan: "keep the hexagon animation where mouse clicks at each circle but make it match the
newer style", "I want it centered at the beginning and it slides to the left and makes space for the text to appear".)

2026-11-07 · long-form / VSL · Don't let one graphic format take over. Rotate the *kind* of visual every
beat: full-screen kinetic type over the speaker blurred (layout `focus`), split screen with the speaker in a
framed window (`split`), objects and UI built straight on the frame with no card, editorial paper / handwriting,
diagrams and full-screen stages. Side glass cards with a checklist are one option among many: never two in a row,
and no more than about one in five graphics. (Bryan: "I feel like we're relying on the same style too much now.")

2026-11-07 · every video · UI sounds should sound like UI, from one matching kit: the `ui-*` cues (uisfx Studio
kit): `ui-open` a card or graphic appears, `ui-select` a small word or option, `ui-expand` a big word, `ui-progress-step`
a list step, `ui-check` a confirm, `ui-press` a tap, `ui-swipe` / `ui-back` full-screen in / out. Not low impact
taps (SFX-0814/0802/0912 sounded like "low impact taps"), not beeps. Never two sounds on one beat. (Bryan: "make
the noises match UI, just not so annoying".) This replaces the low-click palette in the entry below.

2026-11-07 · every video · Keep sound professional: no beeps, dings, chimes or high-pitched UI blips. A low
"thocky" click when a graphic appears (SFX-0814), a dark rounded tock for list steps and confirms (SFX-0802),
a low click for real clicks (SFX-0801), quiet synth swells on full-screen transitions. Literal sounds where the
picture asks for one: cha-ching (`money`) when prices or money land, rope tension (`rubber-stretch`) when a rope
pulls taut. (Bryan: "I don't like the random beeps and high-pitch UI sounds, I want this to feel
professional", "rope stretching noise", "cha-ching noise".)

2026-11-07 · every video · Full-screen backgrounds: never a centred round glow over a dome of light (reads as
the Instagram default profile picture). Use a wide flat glow band along the bottom, soft corner light, faint
texture. Ropes, chains and strings must attach to the thing they hold and move with it. Don't use the
"mellow pluck" (SFX-1389): Bryan hears it as a random bonk. (Bryan: "the background looks like the IG default
profile picture", "fix the anchor rope", "remove the random bonk noise".)

2026-11-07 · every video · Every motion graphic that pops up gets a soft, gentle UI sound (mellow plucks,
pure tones, warm beeps; SFX Library SFX-1389/1373/1388/1372), never aggressive ones. No harsh whooshes:
full-screen transitions get a smooth short synth swell in (SFX-1144) and swish out (SFX-1142), or nothing.
Money on screen gets a money sound (both price tags, not just one). Mute any clap or slate noise before the
first word. (Bryan: "place UI noises that make sense when any motion graphic pops up but not aggressive
sounding ones", "the whooshes sound annoying, not smooth", "remove the clap from the beginning".)

2026-11-07 · every video · No booms (impacts, sub drops, braams, rumble risers, the `hit` cue) and no loud
scratchy glitch noises (library glitches, the `glitch` cue). Clean UI sounds only: soft pops, ticks, glassy
taps, light whooshes. Typing sounds start exactly when the text starts typing and stop when it stops. (Bryan:
"remove the random booms", "remove the loud random scratching noise", "the typing noise came too early".)

2026-11-07 · every video · Go light on sound effects, above all in the opening: only the few big beats
get a sound (about one every 5 s at most in the first 30 s), not every pop, icon and card. Match the sound
to what's on screen literally: a shattering UI gets a UI glass shatter (`ui-glass-shatter`), never an
explosion or a stack of hits. Key-line captions don't always need a hit; leave some silent. (Bryan: "too
many random noises at the beginning", "don't make explosion noise, just UI glass shatter noise", "no
audio with waste of time".)


2026-11-07 · every video · Don't lean on the same effect over and over. Invent new ones as much as
possible: each beat gets a different *kind* of visual (race, gauge, flowchart, phone form, scale,
map, kinetic type...), borrowing ideas from the other styles and REFERENCES.md, but built in the
look the video is being made in. (Bryan: "don't do the same effect a lot, try to create new ones
as much as you can, use inspo from the other styles but in this format.")

2026-11-07 · every video · When Bryan says to go ahead without approving graphics batches, build
them all, check each still yourself, and go straight to the review render.

## YouTube: motion-graphic heavy (2026-10-29, replaces "don't overdo the effects")

2026-10-29 · every 16:9 / YouTube video · Bryan wants a motion-graphic heavy edit: "I want a
lot more motion graphic heavy type of video." Something should be moving almost all the time:
a big graphic scene about every 30-40 s, punch-in cuts on the talking stretches (wide / close
every other sentence), loud kinetic captions on the key lines, and floating cards in between.
Story stretches get scenes that act the story out. The old "about one big effect a minute" and
"leave it out when in doubt" rules below no longer apply; keep only "never the same effect twice
in a row" and "every graphic has a reason in what he's saying."

## YouTube (16:9 long-form): don't overdo the effects (SUPERSEDED 2026-10-29, see "motion-graphic heavy" above)

2026-10-28 · every 16:9 / YouTube video (long-form styles, youtube-clips) · Effects are an
accent, not the edit. A YouTube video is watched for the talk, so most of it is clean
talking head with good captions and cuts. (Bryan: don't overdo how often effects come up.)

- **Big effects** (any Video FX effect, full-frame graphic, B-roll takeover, transition
  effect): about **one a minute at most**, never two within 30 s, and none in a stretch
  where he's telling a story or making an argument; save them for the beats that earn one
  (a number, a list, a reveal, a chapter change).
- **Small moves** (a punch-in, a lower-third, a callout on screen): no more than one every
  ~20 s. Pixel zoom never runs "all the way through" on a YouTube video.
- **Same effect twice:** not within 3 minutes, and no single effect more than 3 times in
  one video.
- **Ideas and combos for YouTube:** pitch fewer, spaced-out ideas (roughly one per minute
  of video). No "max" / "Full send" combo; the levels are **light** (the 3-5 biggest
  beats) and **clean** (1-2 beats plus chapter cards). Every combo's pitch says how many
  effects in how many minutes, e.g. "5 effects across 8 minutes".
- **When in doubt, leave it out.** A plain talking stretch is fine; an effect with no
  reason behind it is not.

Short-form (9:16 Reels, TikTok, Shorts) is unchanged: heavy combos are welcome there.

2026-11-08 · every video · Transitions and text popping up get whooshes that sound like wind, and bullets and list items get a short UI tick on every item: `lst-12` (SFX-1118 Soft Scroll Tick 05) is the one Bryan picked, and lists may repeat it. A process lighting up stage by stage gets `lst-stage` (Ui Click Digital 04, a little louder). An X or a failure gets a wrong-answer buzz (`wrong-buzz`, SFX-1069). Low, pitched thuds and UI taps sound like "an annoying bonk": never use them. Use the real files (assets/sound-effects/Whooshes, the SFX Library), never a few generated sounds or stretched synth swishes (those sounded like a kazoo). Vary them: a wide shuffled pool, no sound twice in a row, no single sound used more than a few times in a video, and leave about half the text pop-ups silent. Replaces the 2026-11-07 "no whooshes" rule. Whooshes stay light, never deep or harsh; list sounds soft and rounded, never low, pitched or booming. (Bryan: "should be airy wooshes and a thud noise when its bullet points or list", "supposed to sound like wind blowing woosh", "how come u dont use the files of sfx we made its always the same sound", "this video has the same sfx too many times".)
